Short Form


Two-page Spread

I chose this spread to recreate because I thought it did a beautiful job incorporating contrast and whitespace. The spread is simple and aesthetically pleasing to the eye, as the only colors apart from black and white are the shades of blue in the image. The large white space on the left page draws attention to the words "Prettiest Boy in the World" and the image, as these are the main aspects of the spread. Contrast is utilized with the stark black words, along with the pop of orange in the author's name. Also, I liked the alignment of this spread, as the largest words are right aligned in order to correlate with the image. When replicating this design, I had to utilize letter and line spacing heavily, especially within the title.

Graphic Design Resume

For this project, I used InDesign to create a Graphic Design Resume. The traditional resume isn't creative at all, but this type of resume is more suited for design/creative jobs. I really worked with text boxes, framing tools, and path tools. I really enjoyed displaying my design talents and knowledge of design principles, as I used contrast, left alignment, and whitespace throughout the whole resume.

Advertisement

For this advertisement, I decided to follow the language of one of Goretex's running shoes campaign -- which personified natural elements as the "enemy". Other advertisements spoke about rain, mud, and snow, so I decided to write about incline. I used similar adjectives in order to personify "incline", and I designed the advertisement in a similar manner as other ads. For example, I placed the image of the shoes very large and towards the top in order to draw the viewer's eye, and used a bold, capitalized font for "INCLINE LOOKS DOWN ON YOU" in order to portray that personified narrative.

Accessibile Design

I created a visual describing "POUR", an acronym used in the marketing field in regard to accessibility, and made the document inclusive and accessible to all. Accessibility is extremely important in the modern marketing field, as most advertisements are digital and/or visual and many disabilities revolve around these fields. To make this piece accessible, I ordered the sections for screen readers, provided alt text, tagged sections, used plain English, and used contrasting colors to make it readible for colorblind people. I used a color palette from the University of Sussex's website, and ran the advertisement through a screen reader to ensure accessibility.

Long Form


Ten-page Spread

I created a 10 page magazine spread with the the theme "Retro Revival", illustrating how Gen-Z is bringing back Y2K fashion. For this project, I used a mix of Photoshop and Indesign to create layers and isolated images in order to create the "Y2K" look. I used the InDesign Lorem Ipsum generator for paragraphs, but created headings, subheadings, and captions using real words in order to indicate my topic. I focused on creating contrast between text and backgrounds, using high quality images, creating visual hierarchy, and choosing contrasting colors for each spread.

Client Project

For this project, I worked with a real client to create promotional materials the Women of Appalachia's performance for WANA's 2025 Writers Conference. First, I sent a Design Questionnare to my client detailing what type of elements he wanted in the materials, such as font families, logos, specific images, etc. I met with my client on Zoom to discuss details, and then created A/B Logo samples for a Facebook post. After meeting again to discuss adjustments, I finalized the Facebook post. I focused on combining the Women of Appalachia colors, like bright green and purple, with the cool colors of WANA in a visually pleasing way. I left-aligned text and ensured contrasting colors for accessibility, while maintaining/combining the vibe of both groups' promotional designs.

UX Design Branding Project

For this project, I created my own brand called "Pick Up n' Play" -- a social media/scheduling app that allows users to connect with others in their area for pick-up games of a variety of sports. For this long form project, I conducted a UX Design Study in order to get real user feedback on the brand's style guide and Logo. The interviews were structured in a way that asked about how the brand's elements matched its identity as well as visual appeal. I created a "Style Guide" consisting of A/B samples of Names, Color Palettes, Mission Statements, and Font Families. I interviewed 3 participants in order to gather feedback, and adjusting my Style Guide accordingly. After this, I created A/B logo samples based on the new Style Guide and conducted another round of interviews with the same participants. After the study concluded, I finalized the final Logo in order, which is pictured above.

4. Practice revision within and across media forms.

I practiced revision the most during the Long Form Projects, as all projects required heavy revision over the course of multiple weeks. For all projects, I started with a draft that displayed my main ideas/hopes for the design, and I constantly revised until I was happy with my creations. Through this portion of the course, I've learned even further the purpose of revision, as having time to sit with my designs allowed me to really "get to know" the piece, which helped me analyze how to improve it. Specifically, I originally focused my 10 Page Spread on "Style through Cities", which illustrated fashion across the world. As I was designing, I found myself continously revising as I was unhappy with the spread. After some time, I realized that my heart wasn't truly in this design theme, and I fully revised the spread to a the Y2K aesthetic. Continuing, I heavily revised my Branding Project after reciving feedback from participants. I revised the color palette according to the feedback, which in turn led to revising my original logo samples. After conducting the study, I realized that it would be best to combine the two logos in order to incorporate their best elements while leaving out the undesirable ones -- such as too much clutter/icons. Without the opportunity for revision, I wouldn't have considered crucial aspects that made the design effective. Finally, I revised my Client Project the most, as I wasn't creating by own design, rather, I was creating materials for an established company. Originally, I created two main A/B samples, but provided around 5 versions of each sample -- each with very slight changes in color, placement, and images. My client ended up preferring Sample A over Sample B, and I revised in order to incorporate his favorite elements from each version into a finalized project. Revision allowed me to create the most effective design for this specific brand, which resulting in both my client and I feeling satisfied. However, I've learned that I'll never feel truly "finished" with my designs, as I beleive there's always room for revision and improvement. For example, I provided my Client with interactive files in order for the design to be further revised in the future. Ultimately, revision is a never-ending cycle for designers, but learning when "enough is enough" is crucial for creating content in a timely, productive, and successful manner.
